# Category D: variable-length FNV 4× manual-unroll probe — 2026-09-14

## Research question

The 2026-09-13 fixed-width microexperiment showed that Mojo 1.0 retained a counted loop for a 32-byte FNV-1a kernel while clang fully unrolled the equivalent C, and that explicitly writing all 32 Mojo steps removed most of that isolated kernel gap. Does that lesson generalize to the **real variable-length FNV loop inside Category D CSV aggregation** strongly enough to explain a meaningful part of the end-to-end Mojo-vs-C gap?

This experiment tests one intervention only: replace the canonical byte-at-a-time Mojo FNV loop with **four sequential FNV steps per loop iteration plus a scalar tail**. The CSV parser, category spans, `List`-backed slots, table capacity, probing, byte equality, aggregation, output reduction, corpus and checksum contract remain unchanged.

## Results

| Architecture | Canonical Mojo mean / median | 4× unroll mean / median | Mean speedup | Median speedup | C mean / median | 4× unroll / C (mean) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Linux x86_64 | 1.074940 / 1.075093 s | 1.010047 / 1.009849 s | **6.42%** | **6.46%** | 0.508449 / 0.507805 s | **1.987×** |
| Linux arm64 | 1.291719 / 1.290166 s | 1.284374 / 1.284795 s | **0.57%** | **0.42%** | 0.417465 / 0.417845 s | **3.077×** |

Measured x86_64 trial dispersion was small relative to the observed intervention: canonical Mojo standard deviation was 0.00458 s and the unrolled variant 0.00413 s. The x86_64 direction is therefore strong within this run. On arm64, the ~0.4–0.6% difference is smaller than ordinary run-to-run scatter here (canonical standard deviation 0.01127 s; unrolled 0.00583 s) and should be treated as **no material improvement**.

For context, before the intervention the same-run canonical-Mojo/C mean ratios were about **2.114× on x86_64** and **3.094× on arm64**. The 4× unroll narrows x86_64 to about **1.987×**, but leaves arm64 essentially unchanged at **3.077×**. It therefore cannot explain the remaining multi-fold Category-D gap.

## Codegen inspection

The emitted assembly confirms that the source intervention survived optimization rather than being rewritten back into the canonical byte-at-a-time shape.

- **x86_64 canonical Mojo:** the FNV hot loop performs one byte load, one `xorq`, one `imulq`, then compares/branches for the next byte.
- **x86_64 4× variant:** the main hash body contains four dependent `xorq`/`imulq` pairs before the chunk-loop condition, followed by a scalar tail loop.
- **arm64 canonical Mojo:** likewise performs one `ldrb` + `eor` + `mul` before the per-byte loop branch.
- **arm64 4× variant:** contains four dependent `eor`/`mul` pairs before the chunk-loop condition, plus a scalar tail.

Whole-file assembly became slightly larger, not smaller: x86_64 grew from 14,078 to 14,301 lines and arm64 from 13,304 to 13,535 lines. That is expected from the extra unrolled/tail control flow and is **not** itself a performance metric. The relevant evidence is the hot hash-loop shape plus the timing intervention.

## Conclusion

**The fixed-trip unrolling lesson only partially generalizes to the real variable-length workload.** Explicit 4× unrolling produces a real, reproducible-within-run **~6.4% end-to-end improvement on Linux x86_64**, so hash-loop control overhead/codegen is a material component there. But the same source/codegen intervention is effectively neutral on Linux arm64, where the remaining Mojo-vs-C gap is larger.

This rejects a stronger hypothesis that “the real Category-D gap is mainly the same missing-unroll problem seen in the fixed 32-byte microkernel.” It is instead architecture- and workload-dependent. The 32-byte microkernel was useful for isolating a compiler transformation, but its ~23–26% intervention effect must not be extrapolated directly to variable-length application code.

The x86_64 result is still useful: it demonstrates an Amdahl-style contribution from the hash loop while leaving roughly a 2× same-run gap to C after the intervention. On arm64, the intervention leaves roughly a 3.08× gap. The next useful work should **decompose parser/delimiter scanning, FNV hashing, probing/equality and aggregation as separate timed or profiled regions**, instead of trying another storage representation or assuming one compiler transform explains the full workload.

## Status

Useful negative/partial result. Keep the experiment and artifacts for reproduction; do **not** promote the 4× variant to canonical Category D from this hosted-runner experiment, and do not change canonical benchmark conclusions.
